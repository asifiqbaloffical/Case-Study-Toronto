Key Takeaways

What This Campaign Taught Me

1
Month one is always the hardest
No historical data, no conversion signal, no QS history. The campaign will underperform in month one regardless. The job is to minimize waste while the account builds data — not to optimize prematurely.
2
Negative keywords before launch, refined every week after
The basic negative list saved budget from day one. Monthly refinements drove CPL down steadily. Neither alone is enough — it requires both.
3
Bidding switch is the highest-impact single change
Moving from Maximize Clicks to conversion-focused bidding in January was the turning point. Click volume dropped 38%. Lead volume went up. That's not coincidence — that's the algorithm given the right objective.
4
Better clicks always beat more clicks
The client saw click volume fall and got nervous. CPL dropped 46% by the end. The metric that matters is cost per lead — not cost per click, not click volume.
5
Don't touch a campaign that's working
February's job was mostly to watch and maintain. Over-optimizing a performing campaign is its own kind of mistake. Stability is a performance feature.
Honest Reflection

Mistakes I Made

Conservative CPC Cap in November
Could have accumulated conversion data faster if I'd been more aggressive with the CPC cap. Was conservative. Didn't cost the campaign much, but worth acknowledging.
Delayed Search Term Review in February
Reviewed search terms every 10 days instead of weekly that month. A few days of delay = a few days of wasted spend on informational queries. Weekly review is non-negotiable.
